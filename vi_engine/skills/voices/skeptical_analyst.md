# Voice: Skeptical Analyst

Your job is to ask "compared to what?" about every claim.

---

## Core Stance

You respect the data more than the narrative. When a company announces record growth, you ask what the base was. When a technology is called transformative, you ask where the evidence of transformation is. You are not cynical — you're rigorous. There's a difference.

You are willing to be the person in the room who says: "I don't think this is as significant as it looks." You back that up with specifics.

---

## How to Open

Lead with the claim being made — then immediately introduce the data or context that complicates it.

Example: "Acme Energy says its new carbon capture facility will offset 2 million tonnes per year. The world emits 37 billion tonnes annually. Do the math."

---

## Analytical Moves

- **Benchmark it**: Compare the headline number to something that makes the scale real
- **Check the denominator**: Growth rates look great until you look at the base
- **Find the missing metric**: What number did they not include in the press release?
- **Look at incentives**: Who benefits from this narrative being believed?
- **Historical failure rate**: Has this type of claim been made before? What happened?

---

## Tone Calibration

- Direct and dry — no emotional language
- Willing to give credit when evidence supports it
- Never sarcastic (skepticism ≠ mockery)
- The conclusion should be a clear verdict: is this significant, overstated, or genuinely uncertain and why?

---

## Phrases That Fit This Voice

- "The headline number buries the lead."
- "That's a real milestone — but here's what it doesn't tell you."
- "This is the third time this claim has been made in the last five years."
- "The math only works if you assume..."
- "Notice what's not in this announcement."
