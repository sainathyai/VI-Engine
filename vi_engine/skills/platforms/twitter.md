# Platform: X (Twitter) Thread

---

## Format Spec

- **Thread length**: 5–8 tweets. No padding. Each tweet must earn its place.
- **Per tweet**: Maximum 280 characters. Aim for 200–260 — leave room for replies and quotes.
- **Thread structure**: Bold opening claim → supporting points → conclusion tweet
- **No "thread 🧵"** opener. No "1/" style numbering in the text (the platform handles threading). No ending tweet that just says "End of thread."

---

## Tweet 1 — The Hook

This tweet is the entire pitch. It must standalone — someone who sees just this tweet should want to read more.

Rules:
- Make a clear, specific, confident claim
- Include a concrete number, name, or fact
- Avoid question openers ("Have you noticed...?") — they're weak
- No emoji-heavy openers

**Bad**: "🧵 Thread on why AI is important for energy companies (1/8)"
**Bad**: "Some thoughts on the EPA's new methane rule..."
**Good**: "The EPA's new methane rule will cost independent Permian operators ~$40K/site/year. Here's what the rule actually requires, who it hits hardest, and what compliance looks like in practice."
**Good**: "Most AI wellhead monitoring pilots are failing — not because the tech doesn't work, but because of this one implementation problem."

---

## Middle Tweets (2–6)

Each tweet = one clear point. Self-contained — assume people might see it out of context.

Patterns that work:
- Specific data point + what it means
- "The part nobody's talking about: [X]"
- Historical parallel in 1–2 sentences
- Direct quote from the source + one-sentence commentary
- "This matters because [specific implication]"

Patterns that don't work:
- Generic observations that could apply to anything
- Tweets that are just transitions ("And that's not all...")
- Anything that requires reading tweets 1–3 to make sense

---

## Final Tweet

Close with one of:
- The clear takeaway: what should the reader think or do differently?
- The unresolved tension: what's the open question that matters most going forward?
- The signal to watch: "The number to track here is X. If it moves past Y, that's when this gets serious."

Do not end with "Follow me for more" or "RT if you agree."

---

## Tone Rules

- Opinion-forward — threads work when they have a clear point of view
- Punchy, not truncated — short sentences by choice, not because you ran out of room
- Dry wit is fine; sarcasm is risky without tone cues
- Don't be afraid to name companies, products, or people directly

---

## Output Format

Return each tweet separated by a blank line and a `---` divider.
No tweet numbers, no emoji labels, no "end of thread" marker.
Each tweet should be ready to post as-is.

Example format:
```
[Tweet 1 text here]

---

[Tweet 2 text here]

---

[Tweet 3 text here]
```
