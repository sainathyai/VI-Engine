# Industry: Oil, Gas & Energy

---

## Who Reads This

Operations leaders, E&P executives, field engineers, energy investors, compliance and HSE teams, midstream asset managers, energy policy analysts. They have been in the industry for years. They know what a Brent/WTI spread means. Don't explain it.

---

## The Core Tensions Driving This Industry Right Now

1. **Energy security vs. energy transition** — The IEA says peak oil demand by 2030. Producers are still sanctioning 30-year capital projects. Someone is wrong.
2. **Capital discipline vs. growth** — Post-2020 shakeout, operators promised shareholders they'd return cash not chase barrels. Prices are high. That promise is under pressure.
3. **ESG pressure vs. operational reality** — Institutional investors want net-zero commitments. Operators in the Permian are flaring because there isn't enough gas pipeline capacity. The gap between the pledge and the physics is enormous.
4. **Technology adoption vs. workforce inertia** — Digital twins, AI-assisted drilling, predictive maintenance — all real and proven. Adoption is still slow because the people who run rigs didn't grow up with software.
5. **Independents vs. majors** — Majors can absorb compliance costs. Independents cannot. Every new regulation draws this fault line sharper.

## Domain Vocabulary (Use Naturally, Don't Define)

**Upstream**: E&P, wellhead, spud, completion, fracking, horizontal drilling, EUR (estimated ultimate recovery), decline curve, Brent, WTI, Henry Hub, OPEC+, production cuts, basin (Permian, Eagle Ford, Bakken, Haynesville), proved reserves

**Midstream**: gathering, processing, fractionation, NGL, LNG terminal, regasification, pipeline tariff, take-or-pay contracts, throughput volumes

**Downstream**: refinery utilization, crack spread, refined products, gasoline/diesel margins, petrochemicals

**Energy transition**: carbon capture (CCS/CCUS), methane emissions, Scope 1/2/3, carbon intensity, SAF (sustainable aviation fuel), hydrogen (grey/blue/green), stranded assets, energy storage, peaker plants

**Finance**: royalty rates, working interest, netback, hedging, D&C (drilling and completion) costs, F&D (finding and development) cost, FCF (free cash flow)

**Regulators**: EPA (US), FERC, DOE, BLM, Texas RRC, BOEM (offshore), IEA, OPEC

## What This Audience Cares About Most

- **Commodity price movements** and what's driving them (geopolitical, inventory, demand signal)
- **Regulatory changes** that create compliance cost or operational constraint
- **Technology that actually reduces opex** — not theoretical efficiency, real cost-per-barrel impact
- **M&A and consolidation** — who is buying whom and what it signals about where the basin is going
- **Workforce and rig count** — leading indicators for production trajectory
- **LNG export capacity** and global gas market dynamics
- **Energy security geopolitics** — Russia/Ukraine, Middle East, US LNG export policy

---

## Takes That Land With This Audience

- The energy transition timeline is based on political targets, not engineering realities — and the industry knows it
- AI in drilling optimization is delivering real results where it's been implemented; the holdback is change management, not technology
- Natural gas is the transition fuel that got sacrificed in the narrative but is essential in the physics
- The Permian's water disposal problem is more existential long-term than its carbon problem
- Small independents will be the casualty of the ESG-driven capital exit — and that's a supply security risk

---

## What to Avoid

- Treating "energy transition" as inherently good or bad — it's complicated
- Confusing "announced capacity" with "operational capacity"
- Citing IEA demand forecasts without noting their track record of revision
- Treating OPEC+ decisions as monolithic — the alliance is fractious
- Using "clean energy" as if it's a self-evident category
