# Industry: Technology

---

## Who Reads This

CTOs, engineering leads, product managers, startup founders, VCs, developer advocates, enterprise IT decision-makers, and technically literate generalists. They read Hacker News. They can spot a press release masquerading as news from the first sentence.

---

## The Core Tensions Driving This Industry Right Now

1. **AI hype vs. AI reality** — The demos are extraordinary. The production deployments are narrower, messier, and more expensive than the demos suggest. The gap is where the real story is.
2. **Open source vs. closed models** — Llama, Mistral, and the OSS ecosystem are closing the capability gap faster than the incumbents expected. The moat is eroding.
3. **Cloud cost vs. cloud dependency** — Companies are now paying more to AWS/Azure/GCP than to headcount. The "cloud is cheaper" era is over for anyone at scale.
4. **Platform power vs. developer trust** — Twitter/X's API shutdown, Reddit's API pricing, Apple's App Store fees — every platform squeeze erodes the trust that made the ecosystem.
5. **AI safety vs. AI capability** — Not an abstract debate. It's driving real decisions about what gets released, when, and with what guardrails.
6. **Foundation model commoditization** — If models are commodities, where is the margin? Infrastructure, data, distribution, and fine-tuning are the battlegrounds.

## What This Audience Cares About Most

- **New model releases** — capabilities, benchmarks, pricing, licensing
- **Developer tooling and ecosystem moves** — new APIs, deprecations, pricing changes that break builds
- **Infrastructure economics** — GPU costs, cloud pricing changes, build vs. buy
- **Regulation** — EU AI Act, US executive orders, open-source AI policy
- **Big company strategy signals** — Microsoft/OpenAI relationship, Google's internal AI tensions, Meta's open-source bet
- **Startup funding and exits** — what sectors are getting capital, what's getting written down
- **Security** — supply chain attacks, model security, data privacy

---

## Takes That Land With This Audience

- Most "AI products" are wrappers on GPT with a $15/mo subscription — that's not a business
- The open-source models closing the gap faster than predicted is the most underreported story in AI
- Cloud cost optimization is now a C-suite conversation — the hyperscaler growth story has a ceiling
- The real AI moat is data, not model architecture — and most companies don't have differentiated data
- Developer experience has become the primary enterprise sales motion — if developers don't love it, the deal dies

---

## What to Avoid

- Treating benchmark performance as equivalent to real-world usefulness
- Conflating "AI" (the broad field) with "LLMs" (one approach)
- Citing funding rounds as proof of product validity
- Treating any "first to market" claim as durable advantage
- Using "disruptive" without explaining what specifically gets disrupted and why
