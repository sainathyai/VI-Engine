# Industry: Financial Technology (FinTech)

---

## Who Reads This

Compliance officers, payments product managers, banking technologists, fintech founders, RegTech analysts, risk managers, financial services investors, and policy watchers. They read CFPB rulings. They know what interchange is. They are skeptical of hype.

---

## The Core Tensions Driving This Industry Right Now

1. **Innovation vs. compliance** — Every new financial product has to run the same gauntlet: KYC/AML, consumer protection, licensing, charter requirements. The cost of compliance is a moat that favors incumbents.
2. **Challenger banks vs. traditional banks** — The challengers got the deposits and the apps. The incumbents have the balance sheets, the regulatory relationships, and the institutional trust. Neobanks are discovering that banking is hard.
3. **Embedded finance vs. platform risk** — Banking-as-a-service let non-banks offer financial products. But BaaS sponsors are now under heavy regulatory scrutiny. The middleware is under pressure.
4. **Crypto integration vs. regulatory clarity** — Stablecoins are real infrastructure. Spot crypto ETFs are approved. But the regulatory framework is still being written and enforcement is inconsistent.
5. **AI in lending vs. fair lending law** — AI credit models can find signal. They can also encode discrimination. Regulators are watching. The explainability requirement is a real technical constraint.
6. **Open banking vs. incumbent lock-in** — PSD2 in Europe forced data portability. In the US, CFPB 1033 is finally moving. Banks are fighting it because their moat is data.

## Domain Vocabulary (Use Naturally, Don't Define)

**Payments**: interchange, payment rails (ACH, wire, RTP, FedNow), card network (Visa/Mastercard/Amex), merchant acquiring, POS, buy now pay later (BNPL), digital wallet, cross-border payments, FX spread, settlement

**Compliance**: KYC (know your customer), AML (anti-money laundering), BSA (Bank Secrecy Act), SAR (suspicious activity report), OFAC sanctions, FinCEN, PCI-DSS, Reg E, Reg Z, fair lending (ECOA, FHA), UDAAP

**Banking**: core banking system, charter (national bank, state bank, ILC), sponsor bank, BaaS (banking-as-a-service), deposit insurance (FDIC), liquidity ratio, capital requirement (Basel III), credit bureau, FICO score

**Lending**: origination, underwriting, default rate, loss reserve, APR, revolving credit, securitization, warehouse line, prime vs. subprime

**Crypto/DeFi**: stablecoin, CBDC, smart contract, custody, on-chain, off-chain, KYC for DeFi, SEC vs. CFTC jurisdiction, spot ETF

**Regulators**: CFPB, OCC, FDIC, Federal Reserve, FinCEN, SEC, CFTC, FCA (UK), EBA (EU)

## What This Audience Cares About Most

- **Regulatory actions** — CFPB enforcement, OCC guidance, Fed rule changes, their effective dates
- **Payment rail evolution** — FedNow adoption rates, RTP volumes, cross-border rail competition
- **Bank-fintech relationship dynamics** — which sponsor banks are pulling back, new partnership structures
- **Credit performance data** — BNPL delinquency rates, consumer credit stress signals
- **Crypto regulatory movement** — stablecoin legislation, ETF flows, SEC enforcement actions
- **M&A** — bank acquisitions of fintechs, fintech consolidation after the rate environment changed
- **AI in financial services** — where it's approved, where regulators are pushing back

---

## Takes That Land With This Audience

- BNPL is subprime credit with better branding — the delinquency data is arriving on schedule
- Open banking in the US is 10 years behind Europe and the incumbents intend to keep it that way
- Most neobanks are still not profitable — they're deposit gatherers living on interchange economics that are being compressed
- The stablecoin moment is real, but the regulatory gap is also real — whoever writes the framework wins
- BaaS is in a reckoning: the sponsor banks got regulatory heat they didn't price in

---

## What to Avoid

- Treating crypto market prices as equivalent to crypto ecosystem health
- Confusing a fintech product launch with a regulatory approval
- Ignoring unit economics when covering "growth" stories
- Treating CFPB enforcement actions as partisan rather than substantive
- Confusing "digital bank" with "neobank" with "challenger bank" — they have different regulatory structures
