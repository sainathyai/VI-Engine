# Industry: Educational Technology (EdTech)

---

## Who Reads This

Curriculum directors, university administrators, instructional designers, edtech investors, K-12 district technology leads, corporate L&D (learning and development) heads, edtech founders, and education policy analysts. They have seen many "this will transform education" cycles. They are appropriately skeptical.

---

## The Core Tensions Driving This Industry Right Now

1. **Engagement vs. learning outcomes** — Platforms optimize for time-on-app and completion rates. Neither correlates reliably with actual learning. This is the industry's fundamental measurement problem.
2. **AI tutor promise vs. classroom reality** — AI tutoring demos look extraordinary. Deploying them in under-resourced classrooms with patchy internet and teachers who weren't consulted is a different story.
3. **Credential inflation vs. credential disruption** — The college degree is under pressure from skills-based hiring — but the degree still dominates hiring for most roles. Micro-credentials are real and growing; they haven't replaced anything yet.
4. **Institutional inertia vs. pace of technology** — K-12 procurement cycles run 18–24 months. LLMs are advancing in months. The gap creates chaos: districts are trying to regulate tools that will be obsolete by the time the policy is written.
5. **Accessibility vs. monetization** — The best edtech tools are expensive. The students who need them most are in districts with the least budget. This is not an oversight — it's a structural problem.
6. **Student data privacy vs. personalization** — Personalized learning requires data. FERPA, COPPA, and state privacy laws constrain what can be collected. AI tutors built on student data face real legal exposure.

## Domain Vocabulary (Use Naturally, Don't Define)

**Platforms & delivery**: LMS (Canvas, Blackboard, Moodle), MOOC (Coursera, edX), adaptive learning, personalized learning path, competency-based education (CBE), synchronous vs. asynchronous, blended learning, flipped classroom

**Credentials**: micro-credential, digital badge, bootcamp, certificate program, accreditation (regional vs. national), credit transfer, prior learning assessment (PLA), stackable credentials

**K-12**: Title I funding, IEP (Individualized Education Program), special education, district procurement, state standards (Common Core, NGSS), standardized testing (NAEP, SAT, AP), EdTech RFP

**Higher ed**: FAFSA, student loan default rate, enrollment cliff, transfer student, dual enrollment, online program management (OPM), revenue sharing

**Corporate L&D**: upskilling, reskilling, skills gap, cohort-based learning, learning management, completion rate, ROI of training

**AI in education**: AI tutor, automated essay scoring, plagiarism detection (Turnitin), AI writing policy, academic integrity, generative AI in curriculum

**Policy**: Department of Education, FERPA (student data privacy), COPPA (children's privacy), Every Student Succeeds Act (ESSA), Title IV funding, accreditor recognition

## What This Audience Cares About Most

- **AI in education** — real deployments, policy responses, academic integrity implications, equity issues
- **Enrollment and demographic trends** — the enrollment cliff, international student policy, community college shifts
- **Funding and market dynamics** — which edtech companies are raising, which are consolidating, what's getting cut
- **Workforce alignment** — which skills are being demanded by employers and whether education is delivering them
- **Policy changes** — Department of Education guidance, state legislation on AI in schools, accreditation changes
- **Learning outcome evidence** — does any of this actually work? RCTs, studies, district-level data

---

## Takes That Land With This Audience

- Most edtech products don't move measurable learning outcomes — and the industry's measurement frameworks are designed to avoid proving that
- The enrollment cliff is not a future problem — it's happening now, and the institutions most exposed are the ones that can't afford to adapt
- AI academic integrity policies written in 2024 are already obsolete — institutions that banned AI are now quietly walking it back
- Skills-based hiring is real at the top of the market (Google, IBM) and mostly irrelevant below it — the degree requirement is sticky
- The OPM model is broken: universities outsourced their online programs and their brand simultaneously

---

## What to Avoid

- Treating completion rates as a proxy for learning
- Confusing venture funding for an edtech company with evidence that the product works
- Presenting "AI in education" as inherently good or bad — the implementation context is everything
- Treating K-12 and higher ed as the same market — they have different buyers, procurement, regulation, and economics
- Citing MOOC enrollment numbers as meaningful without talking about completion (which is typically under 5%)
